# Webstream

## Design Context

### Users
- Public-ish portfolio piece. Strangers may land on it cold and need to "just get it."
- Two surfaces: a laptop browser (viewer) and a phone (sender). Onboarding is the first 10 seconds — it has to feel like a finished instrument, not a dev demo.
- Job-to-be-done: pair phone to browser and start streaming in under 10 seconds, without typing any URL.

### Brand Personality
Three words: **precise, instrument-like, quiet.**

Reference touch points (in spirit, not visual mimicry): Teenage Engineering hardware, Linear's app chrome, the chassis of a high-end camera, the readout of a studio compressor. The tool should feel calibrated — every label considered, every motion deliberate, never showy.

Emotionally: confident, not chatty. Says little, but what it says is exact.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: dark, but warm-tinted — closer to charcoal than midnight. The camera feed is the hero; chrome supports it, never competes.
- **Type**: a quiet grotesque for UI (Hanken Grotesk), JetBrains Mono for technical readouts. NO Inter / IBM Plex / Geist defaults. Small-caps labels with calibrated tracking for status chips.
- **Color**: near-black surfaces tinted slightly toward warm neutral, off-white type, a single warm amber accent (think OP-1 amber, not crypto orange). A single restrained red used exclusively for the live/REC state.
- **Decoration**: instrument-style cues — corner registration marks, tabular numerals for stats, thin hairlines, generous negative space. No gradients, no glass, no rounded glow halos.
- **Motion**: ease-out-quart, never bouncy. Crossfades and translations under 300ms. A slow pulse for "live" status — like a viewfinder REC dot.

### Anti-references
- Glassmorphism / frosted blur (explicitly rejected by user)
- Cyan-on-dark + neon gradients (AI/crypto cliché)
- Linear-clone generic SaaS (Inter + lavender accent + rounded cards)
- Animated-everything maximalism

### Design Principles
1. **The feed is the subject.** Chrome shrinks; video grows. Whitespace around the video frame is sacred.
2. **One accent at a time.** Amber for actionable, red for live, off-white for everything else. No third color.
3. **Calibrate, don't decorate.** A tick mark beats a gradient. A monospace readout beats a sparkline. Show information, not chartjunk.
4. **Pairing is the magic moment.** QR code on the viewer, phone scans, both ends crossfade into live state. Zero typing, zero menus before pairing.
5. **Reduce, then reduce.** If a control isn't load-bearing in the first 10 seconds, hide it behind progressive disclosure.
